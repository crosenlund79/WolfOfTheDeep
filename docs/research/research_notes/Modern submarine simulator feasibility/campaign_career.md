# Dynamic Campaign and Career-Progression Systems for a Modern Submarine Command Simulator

Research date: November 2026. Each claim carries a label:
- **[Fact]** is confirmed by a primary or reliable secondary source (manual, developer, encyclopedia, court ruling, official page).
- **[Dev]** is a developer statement. These are often plans and may have slipped.
- **[Review]** is a critic's opinion.
- **[Community]** is a player or forum report. These are anecdotal and may depend on the game version or mods.
- **[Estimate]** is an approximate figure.

Everything under "Inferences" is my **[Inference]** or **[Recommendation]**, not a sourced finding. "Via search excerpt" marks a source I saw only as a search-result excerpt and could not open in full. Treat those claims with extra caution.

## Q1. Silent Hunter III/4/5: dynamic shipping, patrol orders, renown, flotillas, upgrades, crew, war timeline, and the criticism each received

### Takeaway
SH3 (2005) set the genre benchmark. Its campaign combines a historical war calendar with a spawn layer of randomly generated traffic, a patrol-grid order you can loosely ignore, and a single "renown" currency. Renown pays for boats, upgrades and crew, and drives medals, promotions and career survival. SH4 kept the model and added special missions and aging contact reports, but reviewers noted that it is not a fully simulated war. SH5 swapped depth for a scripted, chapter-like structure and RPG crew abilities, and was panned, mostly for bugs, its interface and DRM.

### Cited Findings
**Silent Hunter III (Ubisoft Bucharest, March 2005)**
- [Fact] There are no scripted missions. The career "simply assigns the player a certain patrol grid, and appropriate naval traffic." Convoys, escorts and task forces are "automatically generated by the simulation engine." — [Wikipedia: Silent Hunter III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_Hunter_III)
- [Fact] The player starts as a junior lieutenant ("Lieutenant, Jr.") and picks a starting U-boat base and date between 1939 and 1943. The war's political and geographic situation at that date frames the career. — [Wikipedia: Silent Hunter III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_Hunter_III)
- [Fact] The game tracks each crew member's rank, experience, morale and decorations between missions, and the crew is visible in a 3D control room. — [Wikipedia: Silent Hunter III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_Hunter_III)
- [Fact] Metacritic 90/100. PC Gamer US and Computer Games Magazine named it "Best Simulation" of 2005. NPD reported 26,600 copies sold by June 2005. — [Wikipedia: Silent Hunter III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_Hunter_III)
- [Review] Subsim says the dynamic campaign "held up release of SH3 for eight months." — [Subsim SH4 page](https://www.subsim.com/sh4/silent_hunter4.php)
- [Community] Patrol orders: you plot a zig-zag across the assigned grid square and stay until the radio confirms enough time has passed, then you are free to hunt elsewhere. — [ratfactor notes](https://ratfactor.com/silent-hunter-iii). Several community sources give the minimum as 24 hours in grid, and say the grid is optional because hunting the known sea lanes earns renown faster. — [Steam SH3 discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/15210/discussions/0/558747287397000048) (via search excerpt)
- [Community] One player reports running out of torpedoes or deck-gun ammunition before reaching the assigned grid. The order structure can clash with logistics. — [Steam SH3 discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/15210/discussions/0/360672047209952740) (via search excerpt)
- [Fact, manual text via search excerpt] Renown is spent as well as earned. "Switching to a new U-Boat or upgrading the conning tower will cost you renown. Improving the systems or adding new ones to your U-Boat will also tax you renown." Also: "If your renown reaches negative values, your career may suddenly end!" — [SH3 manual (Scribd copy)](https://www.scribd.com/doc/44870023/Sh3-Manual-En)
- [Fact, manual text via search excerpt] Flotilla transfer: you can apply "at any time, but your request will be approved only if you proved yourself a valuable U-boat commander." — [SH3 manual (Scribd copy)](https://www.scribd.com/doc/307244348/Sh3-Manual-En)
- [Community] You do not get a better boat just by transferring or by the date advancing. You need enough renown to "purchase" the type once it is available. Boat availability depends on flotilla, date and type, as set in data files. — [Subsim forum archive t-237972](https://www.subsim.com/radioroom/archive/index.php/t-237972.html)
- [Community] Sinking a neutral ship costs renown in career mode. Attacking neutrals makes you a "renegade" to that nation's units for 24 hours. — [Subsim SH3 tips](https://www.subsim.com/tips/tip_sh3.php)
- [Community] Refit time is a logistics cost:
  - each new weapon or subsystem adds 1–3 days in port;
  - a new U-boat adds up to 2 months;
  - a new conning tower adds 3 weeks;
  - each 1% of hull damage adds 1 day of repair;
  - the tips page gives a minimum of 28 days in port between patrols.
  — [Subsim SH3 tips](https://www.subsim.com/tips/tip_sh3.php)
- [Community] Ships follow fixed shipping lanes, busiest around Britain. Resupply at sea was missing at release and was promised for a patch. A 7th Flotilla bug repeatedly announced base changes in 1940, fixed by editing `Flotilla.cfg`. — [Subsim SH3 tips](https://www.subsim.com/tips/tip_sh3.php)
- [Community] Crew fatigue accrues only at time compression of 32x or lower. Exhausted crew stay at their posts until the player sends them to rest. — [ratfactor notes](https://ratfactor.com/silent-hunter-iii). Crew stats include Morale and Fatigue, and fatigue reduces task performance. — [Steam SH3 discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/15210/discussions/0/620712999974154949) (via search excerpt)
- [Community] Renown can hire specialists with set experience and qualifications, for example gunner or repair. Crew earn experience per patrol and become eligible for promotion and medals. — [Subsim forum](https://www.subsim.com/radioroom/showthread.php?p=1952296) (via search excerpt)
- [Community] Over the war, Allied aircraft and radar improve and U-boats increasingly become the hunted. The timeline acts as a built-in difficulty curve. — [ratfactor notes](https://ratfactor.com/silent-hunter-iii)
- [Community] Implementation pattern: the campaign data has a scripted layer (`SCR`, historical units and groups) and a random layer (`Campaign_RND.mis`). Random groups have a "Spawn Probability (%)" and a respawn interval (`DelayMinInterv`), and modders tune convoy frequency by editing these. — [Steam SH3 discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/15210/discussions/0/612823460254704041); [Subsim forum](https://www.subsim.com/radioroom/showthread.php?p=2869507) (via search excerpt)

**Silent Hunter 4: Wolves of the Pacific (2007)**
- [Review] SH4 brings back the dynamic campaign with "special mission objectives such as inserting a spy or commando team into enemy territory" and "historically accurate shipping throughout the region." — [Subsim SH4 page](https://www.subsim.com/sh4/silent_hunter4.php)
- [Review] "Reported contacts age and the actual vessels continue on their course." This is an intelligence-decay mechanic. — [Subsim SH4 page](https://www.subsim.com/sh4/silent_hunter4.php)
- [Review] There is "no random mission generator with SH4." You "can refit and top off at any friendly port." Crew management "has been streamlined" and watches rotate automatically. — [Subsim SH4 page](https://www.subsim.com/sh4/silent_hunter4.php)
- [Review] GameSpot (via search excerpt): the patrol system "hearkens back to the patrol system in Silent Service," but "this isn't a fully dynamic campaign because the game doesn't model a full-scale war all the time." — [GameSpot SH4 review](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/silent-hunter-wolves-of-the-pacific-review/1900-6168086/)

**Silent Hunter 5: Battle of the Atlantic (2010)**
- [Fact] The campaign covers 1939–1943 in a Type VII. The player takes over after the first captain leaves. Metacritic 62. The game shipped with always-online DRM, which a DDoS made unplayable for days in March 2010 and which was relaxed in late 2010. — [Wikipedia: SH5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_Hunter_5:_Battle_of_the_Atlantic)
- [Review, via search excerpt] GameSpot calls it a branching series of assignments into 1943 with "arbitrary and dry" patrol objectives. It says crew morale "frequently plummets to zero for no reason." Another source notes that the earlier detailed crew-management system is gone. — [GameSpot SH5 review](https://gamespot.com/reviews/silent-hunter-5-battle-of-the-atlantic-review/1900-6254133/)
- [Fact, manual via search excerpt] Morale decays at sea and recovers when ships are sunk or objectives completed. The captain's Leadership adds to each crew member's morale. Renown converts to skill points that unlock active and passive crew abilities by veteran level. — [SH5 manual](https://manualmachine.com/ubisoft/silenthunter5/5415755-user-manual)
- [Review] Bit-tech called the role-playing and first-person interior walking "clunky, slow, repetitive and deeply unintuitive," with a very low frame rate in first-person mode. — [Bit-tech SH5 review](https://bit-tech.net/reviews/gaming/pc/silent-hunter-5-review/1/) (via search excerpt)
- [Review] PC Gamer UK said that without the bugs and DRM it "would be the best Silent Hunter yet." 3DJuegos praised its accuracy. — [Wikipedia: SH5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_Hunter_5:_Battle_of_the_Atlantic)

### Inferences
- [Inference] SH3's lasting appeal comes from a few cheap-to-simulate systems working together: a war calendar that changes difficulty and equipment, spawn tables for traffic, one currency (renown) that links combat success to career, refit choices and crew, and refit downtime as a time cost. None of these requires simulating the whole war in real time.
- [Inference] Patrol orders work best as loose framing with a small reward, plus a penalty for embarrassing outcomes such as sinking neutrals. SH3 players treated the grid as a checkbox and then hunted freely. A modern game should make orders matter more without making them rigid. Intelligence-collection orders fit that naturally.
- [Inference] SH5 is a warning: replacing systemic crew and career depth with scripted chapters and RPG ability trees, while shipping technical problems, lost the core audience. The lesson is about priorities. Ship stable core systems before presentation features such as walkable interiors.
- [Inference] SH4's aging contact reports are a cheap, high-value intelligence-decay pattern. It translates directly to modern satellite, P-8 or SOSUS-style cueing.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the full SH3 manual text, so exact renown values, promotion thresholds and medal criteria are unverified. Community sources disagree and many quote mod values (for example GWX).
- I found no developer postmortem for SH3, SH4 or SH5 describing campaign architecture or cost.
- I did not open the GameSpot SH4 and SH5 reviews in full (HTTP 403). Those quotes come from search excerpts.

## Q2. Cold Waters (Killerfish Games, 2017): dynamic campaign structure, consequences, and what players felt was missing

### Takeaway
Cold Waters uses a deliberately compact Red Storm Rising-style campaign. The player moves on a real-time strategic map of the Norwegian Sea and northern Europe, receives time-sensitive mission orders, and meets randomized encounters. Ground-war icons and news flashes show the wider war, and the player's results move it modestly. Reviewers found the campaign layer "perfectly fine" but thin and wooden. The main criticisms were a limited map view, repetition, no after-action replay, no crew or career depth, and a developer that went quiet after about 2018.

### Cited Findings
- [Fact] The player commands a US submarine in 1968, 1984 or 2000 during a hypothetical WWIII against the Soviet Union or the PRC. The game is described as a spiritual successor to MicroProse's *Red Storm Rising* (1988). — [Wikipedia: Cold Waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_Waters_(video_game))
- [Fact] The Steam store lists "Dynamic Campaign where your performance matters," over 40 researched ship and submarine classes, and convoy, amphibious-landing and insertion tasks. Reviews: 87% positive of 3,523 English reviews ("Very Positive"). — [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/541210/Cold_Waters/)
- Release date conflict: the Steam store lists 5 June 2017 — [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/541210/Cold_Waters/) — while Wikipedia lists 5 July 2017 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_Waters_(video_game)). A v1.02b beta thread dated 14 June 2017 supports the June date. — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/2119355556473234455)
- [Review] At launch there were two campaigns, 1968 and 1984, each with era-correct ships, weapons and AI tactics. "News flashes update the player on the events of the conflict." On the strategic map, aircraft and satellite icons move, "tanks stake out ground on the continent," and the sub moves at patrol speed (15 kn) or full speed (25 kn) to intercept convoys or hunter-killer groups. — [Subsim review](https://www.subsim.com/ssr/cold_waters/review_cold_waters.php)
- [Review] "The player is given a time-sensitive mission." Damage persists, and some repairs "could only be made in port." — [Subsim review](https://www.subsim.com/ssr/cold_waters/review_cold_waters.php)
- [Review] "Everything that Red Storm Rising's campaign does, Cold Waters' campaign does also." The map cannot scroll, so it is hard to tell how the war is going. Soviet progress shows as "static tank icons representing control over various regions that flip from blue to red." "The campaign layer is perfectly fine." Much of play is "waiting around staring at the sonar map with time compression turned on." Red Storm Rising's after-action replay is missing. — [Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/thoughts-cold-waters/)
- [Review, via search excerpt] Another review says sinking enough Soviet submarines stalls or pushes back the red advance shown on the map. — reported in search results alongside [Scientific Gamer](https://scientificgamer.com/thoughts-cold-waters/) (I could not pin down the exact page)
- [Dev] Campaign patch notes (2017):
  - fixed "general campaign missions with bad parameters";
  - fixed insertion missions;
  - removed sonobarriers from ports as "undetectable and not fun";
  - noisemakers now replenish and hull damage is repaired on entering port.
  A player asked for a "try to avoid" option when the strategic map triggers a contact. — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/1353742967812606761)
- [Dev] Update 1.15b (June 2018) added a quick mission editor and single missions for Soviet and Chinese subs. A player noted there were only six non-US single missions. — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/1726450077624204408)
- [Community] By December 2019, players complained of "a year and a half since any update." A former Killerfish co-founder posted that he and the art team had "formed a new studio" and had "no idea what they are currently working on." — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/1744513943986905764)
- [Community] A user review says the campaign map "plays very wooden." — [RAWG reviews](https://rawg.io/games/cold-waters/reviews)

### Inferences
- [Inference] Cold Waters shows that a very small team can ship a well-received "dynamic" campaign by keeping the strategic layer abstract: a scripted war-event timeline, region-ownership flips, a mission queue and spawned encounters. Most of the effort goes into the tactical layer. It is the closest proven model to an MVP for this project.
- [Inference] What players missed points to cheap, high-value additions: crew and career persistence, campaign memory such as an after-action log, more legible consequences, avoid or evade choices on the strategic map, and more mission variety beyond "sink X before time T."
- [Inference] A modern sub game should avoid making peacetime mostly a time-compression watch. Mission design must create decisions such as risk of counter-detection, ROE limits, or collection quality, not just waiting.

### Gaps
- I found no Killerfish developer postmortem or interview explaining the campaign's internal logic: how mission outcomes feed the war-progression meter, the threshold for the final mission, or the win and loss conditions.
- A search excerpt attributed to a Killerfish reply the claim that every campaign mission comes from a "random mission generator." I could not verify it in the threads I opened, so I am not reporting it as fact.
- I could not fetch the full PC Gamer review text.

## Q3. The Falcon 4.0 / BMS dynamic campaign as the living-world benchmark, its cost and scope history, and DCS efforts (Liberation/Retribution, Eagle Dynamics' own) plus Sea Power

### Takeaway
Falcon 4.0's campaign is the reference design. It runs as a background strategy game with strategic, operational and tactical AI, and missions fall out of target priorities. Units are aggregated outside a fidelity "bubble" around the player. It took about five years and around 50 people over the project's life, shipped buggy and rushed, ran on about 5% of the CPU, and probably did not make money. Its lead engineer would now mix scripted and generated missions. Later attempts confirm the cost. Eagle Dynamics' own dynamic campaign has been in development for about five years without shipping, and Sea Power's has slipped repeatedly. Community tools such as DCS Liberation and Retribution get most of the value with a cheaper turn-based design.

### Cited Findings
**Falcon 4.0 (MicroProse, 1998)**
- [Dev] Lead engineer Kevin Klemmick designed the campaign like a strategy game that runs in the background whether or not the player flies. It has three AI tiers, strategic, operational and tactical, plus a fourth level driving vehicles. The directive was "to make something that would be a persistent world and generate dynamic missions." — [Falcon 4 history: Klemmick interview](https://sites.google.com/site/falcon4history/interview)
- [Dev] Missions were "a byproduct of the AI." Once target priorities were set, strike packages were built with real-world planning: SEAD, air-superiority escort, tankers and AWACS timed together. — [Klemmick interview](https://sites.google.com/site/falcon4history/interview)
- [Dev] [Estimate] Team and time:
  - about 50 people over the project, with two team turnovers;
  - roughly 6 engineers and a similar number of artists for most of it, and at one point Klemmick was the only programmer;
  - about 5 years of development, with the last 9 months at 12–16 hour days.
  — [Klemmick interview](https://sites.google.com/site/falcon4history/interview)
- [Dev] The campaign had about a 5% CPU budget, which forced compromises in AI quality ("all of this had to work within a very tiny slice of the CPU"). Modules built in isolation used three different coordinate systems. Complexity and the lack of a central design made bugs hard to find. — [Klemmick interview](https://sites.google.com/site/falcon4history/interview)
- [Dev] Klemmick suspects MicroProse did not profit because development cost was well above average. He says: "if I were to do it over I would do a mix of scripted/generated missions," and dynamic campaigns "score pretty low" on cost versus benefit, which is why few modern sims attempt them. — [Klemmick interview](https://sites.google.com/site/falcon4history/interview)
- [Fact] Development began in 1994. The game was originally slated for late 1996 and was reportedly rushed out for Christmas 1998 (a claim Wikipedia tags as needing a citation). It released on 11 December 1998, scored Metacritic 85, and sold about 209,000 copies during 1999. — [Wikipedia: Falcon 4.0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon_4.0)
- [Fact] The source code leaked in April 2000. Community branches (FreeFalcon, Benchmark Sims) continued it. Falcon BMS 4.38 released on 1 July 2025. MicroProse reacquired the Falcon IP in May 2023. — [Wikipedia: Falcon 4.0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon_4.0)
- [Community] The campaign was still buggy at release and stabilized only with later patches. — [SimHQ forum](https://simhq.com/forum/ubbthreads.php/posts/4300835) (via search excerpt)
- [Community] BMS "bubble" deaggregation: units near the player are de-aggregated into individual high-fidelity 3D entities, and units outside run as aggregated 2D entities, because the whole strategic war cannot run in 3D. Losses taken while aggregated carry over, so de-aggregation spawns only survivors. One forum post cites bubble sizes from BMS documentation: about 19 nm for aircraft, 8 nm for helicopters and naval units, 4 nm for ground units, and SAM range plus 10%. Weapon launches create temporary bubbles. Posters disagree on details. — [Falcon BMS forum: Player Bubble and Deaggregation](https://forum.falcon-bms.com/post/78101)
- [Dev] As of November 2025, Razbam's CEO teased that the "design phase of Falcon 5's Dynamic Campaign was completed this summer," in a post later deleted. The MicroProse/Razbam Falcon 5 project is unconfirmed beyond that. — [Simulation Daily](https://simulationdaily.com/news/falcon-5-dynamic-campaign-tease/)

**DCS: Eagle Dynamics' official dynamic campaign**
- [Dev] In March 2025 the design was built around the ATO, with a strategic AI that generates missions from battlefield priorities. Players can let missions generate automatically or steer the campaign's focus, take over any flight, and interdict logistics modeled as supply vehicles moving on the map. Matt Wagner said development took longer than expected because of the "massive scope." — [FSElite, Mar 2025](https://fselite.net/content/dcs-world-qa-reveals-2025-roadmap-and-dynamic-campaign-progress/)
- [Dev] In January 2026 the roadmap described the dynamic campaign as in development for "approximately five years." Work focused on strategic and tactical AI, strike-package logic and a client-side ATO and objective interface. No release date was given. — [FSElite, Jan 2026](https://fselite.net/content/eagle-dynamics-reveals-2026-roadmap-with-focus-on-dynamic-campaign-and-vulkan-api/)
- [Dev, via search excerpt] Eagle Dynamics is working on aggregating and de-aggregating units for large-battle performance, the same bubble pattern as Falcon. — [Simulation Daily progress update](https://simulationdaily.com/news/dcs-world-dynamic-campaign-progress-update/)

**DCS Liberation / Retribution (community, open source)**
- [Fact] Retribution describes itself as a "turn based single-player or co-op dynamic campaign," an external program that generates complete DCS missions and keeps a persistent combat environment between turns. It uses `pydcs` for mission generation, Skynet-IADS for air defense and Mist for scripting. — [DCS Retribution README (fork mirror)](https://github.com/Chilli935/dcs-retribution)
- [Fact] DCS AI bugs limit the design. A2A and SEAD escorts do not escort properly, and save compatibility is broken across builds, with migrations, so the README advises backing up saves by version. — [DCS Retribution README](https://github.com/Chilli935/dcs-retribution)
- [Community] Each turn the player plans packages (CAP, strike, SEAD) and exports a `.miz` file that represents one turn. The campaign save is the "source of truth." Time stops between turns. Retribution forked from Liberation in 2022. — [FlyAwaySimulation guide](https://flyawaysimulation.com/ask/answers/dcs-liberation-vs-dcs-retribution/); [DCS forum thread](https://forum.dcs.world/topic/368593-dcs-retribution-dynamic-campaign-generator) (via search excerpt)

**Sea Power (Triassic Games / MicroProse), a modern naval sim**
- [Dev, via search excerpt] October 2024 roadmap: a dynamic campaign alpha targeted for Q2 2025, explicitly inspired by Falcon 4.0's campaign. — [MicroProse: Sea Power roadmap](https://www.microprose.com/news/sea-power-early-access-roadmap)
- [Dev, via search excerpt] A November 2025 revision moved the dynamic campaign to Q2 2026. The Steam page later listed a "theater scale (beta version launching mid 2026) Dynamic Campaign" and a 1.0 target of Q4 2026. — [Simulation Daily roadmap](https://simulationdaily.com/news/sea-power-early-access-roadmap/); [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1286220/Sea_Power/)
- [Dev] June 2026: Triassic is taking a "pragmatic approach." It is first building supporting systems (persistence across scenarios, force construction, damage and repair management, AI), a semi-persistent "Task Force" mode and a linear/persistent Falklands 1982 campaign. The stated goal is a core campaign loop that is "playable, testable, and expandable." — [Stormbirds, Jun 2026](https://stormbirds.blog/2026/06/05/sea-power-lays-out-updated-roadmap-with-new-falklands-scenarios/)

### Inferences
- [Inference] Each full Falcon-style living-world campaign has taken years, with a 5-year original build in 1994–98, about 5 years and counting at Eagle Dynamics, and repeated Sea Power slips. A small team should not attempt a continuously simulated theatre war as a first deliverable.
- [Inference] Triassic's staging is the most transferable lesson: persistence, then a linear persistent campaign, then semi-persistent task-force operations, then a dynamic campaign. Each step is shippable and tests the save model and damage carry-over before AI planning is added.
- [Inference] The turn-based "campaign state → generate one encounter → read back results" loop used by Liberation and Retribution decouples strategic logic from the real-time tactical engine. In Godot it could keep the campaign layer in plain, testable scripts or Resources, separate from scene nodes, which also matches this repository's architecture guideline.
- [Inference] Bubble or aggregation is needed even in a much smaller game. Off-screen ships should move as abstract tracks on a strategic graph and become full tactical entities only within sensor or engagement range.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable dollar budget for Falcon 4.0 or its campaign. Forum claims that it "bankrupted MicroProse" are unsourced.
- I could not confirm when Eagle Dynamics first announced its dynamic campaign, and I found no confirmation that it has shipped as of November 2026.
- I could not confirm whether Sea Power's dynamic campaign beta shipped in mid-2026 as planned, or what its final architecture is.

## Q4. Other references (briefly): UBOAT, Command: Modern Operations, Rule the Waves, Silent Service, FTL-style maps, and career modes in other sims

### Takeaway
Each reference covers a different slice:
- UBOAT shows crew needs and logistics tied to a war-state supply economy and varied non-combat missions.
- Command: Modern Operations shows that a deep modern sim can be purely scenario-based.
- Rule the Waves shows a career built on tension, budget and political influence rather than direct control.
- FTL shows a compact node-map campaign with pursuit pressure.
- IL-2 Great Battles and Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024 show generated-mission careers with reputation and certification gates.

### Cited Findings
**UBOAT (Deep Water Studio / PlayWay)**
- [Review] Campaigns run from 1941 to late 1944. Officers have specific abilities and sailors are generalists, a mix the reviewer calls "hints of XCOM with a sprinkling of The Sims." Morale and fatigue erode on long patrols, and sinkings boost morale. Panicking crew can make noise. Food quality affects morale. — [VICE review](https://www.vice.com/en/article/uboat-recasts-the-sub-sim-as-a-roguelike-management-game)
- [Review] Reputation acts as currency for materiel, personnel and upgrades, but supply depends on how the war is going: "if the war's going badly no amount of goodwill and prestige will conjure diesel out of thin air." — [VICE review](https://www.vice.com/en/article/uboat-recasts-the-sub-sim-as-a-roguelike-management-game)
- [Review] Missions come from a Kriegsmarine envoy and go beyond sinking ships: landing spies, infiltrating ports, sending teams ashore, carrying war correspondents. The game is "starkly de-Nazified," and sailors "fight the war in a bottle, utterly unaffected by the wider world." An Early Access update reset saves, and a 1944 campaign opened with 1941 headlines. — [VICE review](https://www.vice.com/en/article/uboat-recasts-the-sub-sim-as-a-roguelike-management-game)
- [Review] Repairs happen automatically if enough spare parts are aboard, so parts stock is a logistics concern. Players track fuel, electric power, morale and air quality. — [GameGrin review](https://www.gamegrin.com/reviews/uboat-review) (via search excerpt)

**Command: Modern Operations (Matrix/Slitherine)**
- [Fact] The game is scenario-based, with remastered official scenarios, community scenarios and a scenario editor. The Wargamer review mentions no dynamic campaign or career. — [Wargamer CMO review](https://www.wargamer.com/reviews/command-modern-operations/)
- [Community] One forum user observes that the AI "is not trying to solve problems. It is trying to follow orders," so challenge depends on the scenario designer. — [Matrix Games forum](https://forums.matrixgames.com/viewtopic.php?p=5241125) (via search excerpt)

**Rule the Waves 3 (Naval Warfare Simulations)**
- [Dev] The player is "Grand Admiral" from 1890 to 1970 (optionally to 1980). The budget varies with tension: "in times of high tension it will tend to rise." "You can influence but not decide foreign policy," and "Hawkish answers will tend to drive up tension and increase the navy budget." War can start without warning. Battleships take years to build and may be obsolete on commissioning. — [Slitherine dev diary #1](https://www6.slitherine.com/news/rule-the-waves-3-dev-diary-1-what-is-rule-the-waves)
- [Review, via search excerpt] Rule the Waves 2 has "a wonderfully stressful gamble between actually putting ships in the water and chasing after the latest tech." — [Wargamer RTW2 review](https://www.wargamer.com/reviews/rule-the-waves-2/)

**Silent Service (MicroProse, 1985)**
- [Fact] Designed by Sid Meier, it covers US war patrols against Japanese shipping. It sold 250,000 copies by March 1987 (about 400,000 overall). — [Wikipedia: Silent Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_Service_(video_game))

### Inferences
- [Inference] A modern-sub analogue to MSFS-style certification gates is qualification for boat classes and mission types: SSN versus SSGN, special-operations insertion, under-ice operations. Reputation would gate sensitive taskings. This replaces SH3's purchase-a-boat-with-renown model, which would be implausible in a modern navy.
- [Inference] UBOAT's critique ("war in a bottle") and Rule the Waves' tension-and-budget model suggest one global "tension / readiness" variable. It could drive mission mix, ROE strictness, resupply priority and news flavour, and would make the wider world feel present cheaply.
- [Inference] FTL's node map plus pursuit timer suggests a compact patrol structure. A patrol becomes a graph of areas or waypoints with a clock (endurance, food, crew fatigue, or a deadline) instead of a free continuous ocean, which cuts content cost sharply.
- [Inference] Command: Modern Operations shows that scenario-only design is commercially viable for modern naval sims. A scenario editor plus a light career wrapper is a legitimate fallback if a dynamic campaign proves too costly.

## Q5. Design patterns: layered simulation, bubble/LOD, procedural missions, intelligence decay, logistics, crew, career, persistence and saves

### Takeaway
The proven pattern stacks three layers:
1. Strategic theatre state, a small set of variables and region ownership.
2. Operational traffic generation, using spawn tables, scripted historical or doctrinal groups, and abstract tracks.
3. Tactical encounters, fully simulated only inside a sensor or engagement bubble.

Missions are templates instantiated from theatre state. Intelligence is a set of contact reports that age. Logistics and crew are resource meters that limit patrol length. The career turns outcomes into reputation and qualifications. The academic literature on simulation level of detail (LOD) and quest generation formalizes these patterns.

### Cited Findings
- [Dev] Layered AI, strategic → operational → tactical → vehicle: Falcon 4.0. — [Klemmick interview](https://sites.google.com/site/falcon4history/interview)
- [Fact/Community] Spawn-table traffic layers: SH3's scripted (`SCR`) and random (`RND`) campaign layers, with group spawn probability and respawn interval. — [Steam SH3 discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/15210/discussions/0/612823460254704041)
- [Community] Bubble or aggregation with loss carry-over, as in Falcon BMS. — [Falcon BMS forum](https://forum.falcon-bms.com/post/78101)
- [Fact] Simulation LOD in the literature (each described from its abstract or search excerpt):
  - Chenney (2001) defines simulation level-of-detail as reducing AI and physics cost without much loss in behaviour quality, using "proxy simulations" (cheap discrete-event stand-ins that produce the same player-visible events) — [Chenney, "Simulation Level-Of-Detail"](https://www.academia.edu/52854015/Simulation_Level_Of_Detail);
  - Brom, Šerý & Poch (2007) argue for several graded fidelity levels rather than a binary switch — [Brom et al. 2007](https://core.ac.uk/works/16904818);
  - Diamanti & Thue (AIIDE 2019) propose automatic abstraction and refinement for adaptive LOD — [AIIDE 2019](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/5219).
- [Fact] Procedural missions: Doran and Parberry analysed over 750 quests from four MMORPGs and found a common structure. Quest patterns carry implicit preconditions and postconditions, so a generator can elaborate subquests while those conditions hold. — [Doran & Parberry, PCG 2011](https://ianparberry.com/pubs/pcg2011.pdf)
- [Review] Intelligence decay: in SH4, "Reported contacts age and the actual vessels continue on their course." — [Subsim SH4 page](https://www.subsim.com/sh4/silent_hunter4.php)
- [Dev] Logistics as gameplay:
  - Eagle Dynamics models supply vehicles moving on the map, which makes interdiction a mission type — [FSElite](https://fselite.net/content/dcs-world-qa-reveals-2025-roadmap-and-dynamic-campaign-progress/);
  - UBOAT links supply availability to the war situation — [VICE](https://www.vice.com/en/article/uboat-recasts-the-sub-sim-as-a-roguelike-management-game);
  - SH3 charges refit time in days and weeks — [Subsim SH3 tips](https://www.subsim.com/tips/tip_sh3.php).
- [Fact/Review] Crew systems:
  - SH3 tracks individual rank, experience, morale and decorations — [Wikipedia SH3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_Hunter_III);
  - SH5 has morale decay at sea, a leadership bonus and ability trees — [SH5 manual](https://manualmachine.com/ubisoft/silenthunter5/5415755-user-manual) (via search excerpt);
  - UBOAT splits officers from sailors and adds food, fatigue and panic noise — [VICE](https://www.vice.com/en/article/uboat-recasts-the-sub-sim-as-a-roguelike-management-game).
- [Dev/Fact] Persistence and save risk:
  - Retribution breaks save compatibility across builds and migrates saves — [Retribution README](https://github.com/Chilli935/dcs-retribution);
  - UBOAT reset saves during Early Access — [VICE](https://www.vice.com/en/article/uboat-recasts-the-sub-sim-as-a-roguelike-management-game);
  - Killerfish stated that a patch "Shouldn't break any saves," which shows players care — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/1353742967812606761).
- [Community] Crew-system exploits: SH3 fatigue does not accrue above 32x time compression, so the simulation can be gamed through time compression. — [ratfactor](https://ratfactor.com/silent-hunter-iii)

### Inferences
- [Recommendation] Proposed minimum layered architecture, for user review and not an approved design:
  - (a) **TheatreState**: date; global tension or DEFCON-like level; region control; force posture per faction, held as counts or abstract groups; a handful of event flags.
  - (b) **TrafficDirector**: weighted spawn tables per region, date and tension, producing abstract tracks on a route graph (merchant lanes, patrol boxes, SSBN bastions). Each track carries a position, course, speed and a "truth" record.
  - (c) **IntelPicture**: the player-facing contact reports derived from truth with noise, a timestamp and a confidence that decays with age. Reports get no positional update unless re-detected.
  - (d) **EncounterInstantiator**: when a track enters the player's sensor bubble, spawn a full tactical entity. On leaving the bubble, collapse it back to a track and keep its damage and losses.
  - (e) **MissionGenerator**: templates with preconditions (tension level, boat qualification, region) and parameters (target, area, time window, ROE). Results write back into TheatreState and the career.
- [Recommendation] Logistics MVP: weapons inventory, a fixed food and stores endurance (nuclear) or fuel plus battery (diesel), and a damage-to-refit-days conversion as in SH3. Track spare parts only if a later scope decision asks for it.
- [Recommendation] Crew MVP: department-level crew (sonar, weapons, engineering, control) instead of individual sailors. Each department has skill, fatigue and morale. Name individual officers only, so key characters can be promoted or lost. Fatigue should accrue in abstract time regardless of time compression, to avoid SH3's exploit.
- [Recommendation] Save model: one serializable campaign-state document, versioned from day one with an explicit migration step. The tactical scene state is not saved mid-engagement initially, which avoids the hardest serialization work. Several projects broke saves on updates.
- [Inference] Doran and Parberry's precondition and postcondition framing maps cleanly onto naval mission templates. For example, "collect acoustic signature of class X" requires that class to be deployed in a reachable region and produces an intelligence gain plus a tension change if the boat is counter-detected.

### Gaps
- I did not read the full texts of the LOD papers or of Doran and Parberry. Descriptions come from abstracts and search excerpts.
- I found no published technical breakdown of SH3's internal campaign scheduler beyond community file editing.
- I found no academic source specifically on intelligence-uncertainty decay models for games. The SH4 example is a design precedent, not a formal model.

## Q6. Modern-era framing: peacetime and crisis missions, ROE as tension, and real-world versus fictional settings (sensitivity, longevity, licensing)

### Takeaway
Real Cold War submarine practice offers rich non-shooting gameplay. Trailing missions, intelligence collection and tense ROE-governed encounters can escalate through miscommunication, and SH4 and UBOAT already show special-operations insertion and spy landings as mission types. For setting, US trademark law currently favours depicting real military hardware in expressive works (AM General v. Activision, 2020). Real geography and politics carry sensitivity risks: Bohemia's developers were arrested in Greece and the island was renamed, and SH5 was recalled in Germany. Fictionalized or near-future framings reduce these risks and age better.

### Cited Findings
- [Fact] During the Cold War, USS *Batfish* trailed a Soviet Yankee-class SSBN for 51 hours over 350 nautical miles before losing contact in a storm. Its skipper described the task as establishing "a trailing position well behind the Yankee from which we could maintain tactical control." Rear Adm. Sumner Shapiro believed that Soviet awareness of such trailing undermined confidence in SSBN invulnerability. — [Smithsonian Magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/run-silent-run-deep-72432889/)
- [Fact] During the 1962 B-59 incident, the ROE required intercepting destroyers to close aboard and hoist "heave to immediately" signals while trying radio contact. A miscommunication nearly triggered wider war. — [USNI Naval History, Oct 2026](https://www.usni.org/magazines/naval-history/2026/october/one-dark-night-sargasso-sea)
- [Fact, via search excerpt] A study of peacetime incidents argues that routine naval missions "almost always have stratified escalation dynamics, misperceptions, and political-military tensions." It focuses on escalation-inhibiting features and the conditions under which they break down. — [DTIC report ADA234363](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA234363.pdf) (attribution to this specific PDF unverified)
- [Review] SH4 added insertion of spies and commandos — [Subsim SH4 page](https://www.subsim.com/sh4/silent_hunter4.php). UBOAT has spy landings, port infiltration and correspondent transport — [VICE](https://www.vice.com/en/article/uboat-recasts-the-sub-sim-as-a-roguelike-management-game). Cold Waters has insertion and land-strike missions — [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/541210/Cold_Waters/).
- [Fact] AM General v. Activision (S.D.N.Y., 2020): the court ruled that Call of Duty's depiction of Humvees was protected expressive use under the Rogers v. Grimaldi test, reasoning that realism is a legitimate artistic goal. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/judge-rules-that-call-of-duty-can-have-humvees-in-it-because-games-are-art/); [Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/thr-esq/call-duty-wins-first-amendment-victory-use-humvees-1287882)
- [Fact] In September 2012, two Bohemia Interactive employees were arrested on Lemnos and charged with espionage for allegedly photographing military installations. They were held 129 days and later given suspended sentences. Bohemia renamed the game island from Lemnos to "Altis" "to emphasize the game's fictional nature." Arma 3 uses a near-future 2035 setting with a fictional bloc (CSAT) based on the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation. — [Wikipedia: Arma 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arma_3)
- [Fact] The SH5 Collector's Edition was recalled in Germany over WWII symbols. — [Wikipedia: SH5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_Hunter_5:_Battle_of_the_Atlantic)
- [Fact] Cold Waters uses real nations (USSR and PRC) in a hypothetical WWIII set in 1968, 1984 and 2000, which avoids depicting a present-day war. — [Wikipedia: Cold Waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_Waters_(video_game))
- [Fact] Command: Modern Operations simulates "every military engagement from post World War II to the present day and beyond" with real platforms. — [Steam store: CMO](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1076160) (via search excerpt)

### Inferences
- [Recommendation] Peacetime and crisis mission families that cost little to generate from the layered model:
  - trail and track (hold contact for N hours without counter-detection);
  - signature and intelligence collection (get within range, record, and survive undetected);
  - surveillance of a port or chokepoint (count departures);
  - special-operations insertion and extraction;
  - shadowing a surface group;
  - escalation-ladder missions where the ROE loosen as theatre tension rises.

  Counter-detection should raise tension and lower reputation, which ties tactical stealth to strategic consequence.
- [Inference] ROE is a strong tension source. Weapons-release authority that depends on tension, with penalties for violations as in SH3's renown loss for sinking neutrals, creates dilemmas without new simulation systems.
- [Recommendation] Setting trade-off:
  - A real-world present-day setting is the most authentic, but carries sensitivity risk (real ongoing conflicts, regional bans), ages quickly as geopolitics shift, and may raise naming or likeness questions. US law currently favours depicting real hardware, but that is jurisdiction-specific and not legal advice.
  - A historical "what-if" setting (Cold War 1980s, like Cold Waters and Red Storm Rising) or a near-future fictional bloc (like Arma 3) ages better. Real geography can stay while factions and events are fictionalized.
  - Choosing the setting is a user decision. It is undecided in the project brief.
- [Inference] Do not do real-world photographic reference gathering near military sites, given the Arma 3 incident.

- I found no developer postmortem quantifying how a real-world setting affected a naval sim's sales longevity or platform approval.
- I did not verify which DTIC document contains the "stratified escalation" passage.

## Q7. Scope risks: which campaign features historically ballooned, and how to stage a minimum viable but extensible campaign for a small team

### Takeaway
The features that ballooned were:
- full living-world simulation (Falcon 4.0: about 5 years and about 50 people; Eagle Dynamics: about 5 years without shipping; Sea Power: repeated slips);
- a dynamic campaign added late in development (SH3: an eight-month delay);
- RPG and walking-interior presentation layered on an unstable core (SH5);
- save compatibility across updates (Retribution, UBOAT).

Small teams that succeeded (Cold Waters, FTL) kept the strategic layer abstract and focused effort on one core experience.

### Cited Findings
- [Review] The dynamic campaign "held up release of SH3 for eight months." — [Subsim SH4 page](https://www.subsim.com/sh4/silent_hunter4.php)
- [Dev] Falcon 4.0 took about 5 years with about 50 people over the project's life. Integration problems came from three coordinate systems and no central design. The game probably lost money, and its engineer would now use mixed scripted and generated missions. — [Klemmick interview](https://sites.google.com/site/falcon4history/interview); its development started in 1994 and it shipped in December 1998, reportedly rushed — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon_4.0)
- [Dev] The Eagle Dynamics dynamic campaign has been in development "approximately five years," with no release date as of January 2026 — [FSElite](https://fselite.net/content/eagle-dynamics-reveals-2026-roadmap-with-focus-on-dynamic-campaign-and-vulkan-api/). Development took longer than expected because of its "massive scope" — [FSElite](https://fselite.net/content/dcs-world-qa-reveals-2025-roadmap-and-dynamic-campaign-progress/).
- [Dev] The Sea Power dynamic campaign slipped from an alpha target of Q2 2025 to a beta target of mid-2026. The developer then staged persistence, task-force and linear campaigns first. — [Simulation Daily](https://simulationdaily.com/news/sea-power-early-access-roadmap/); [Stormbirds](https://stormbirds.blog/2026/06/05/sea-power-lays-out-updated-roadmap-with-new-falklands-scenarios/)
- [Review] SH5's RPG and interior-walking features were judged "clunky, slow, repetitive," and the game shipped buggy with poorly received DRM. — [Bit-tech](https://bit-tech.net/reviews/gaming/pc/silent-hunter-5-review/1/) (via search excerpt); [Wikipedia SH5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_Hunter_5:_Battle_of_the_Atlantic)
- [Community] Cold Waters went about 18 months without updates by late 2019, and the original team split. — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/541210/discussions/0/1744513943986905764)
- [Fact] FTL's two-person team succeeded by cutting anything that did not fit one focus, and initially dropped multiplayer. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-without-a-pitch---an-em-ftl-em-postmortem)

### Inferences
- [Recommendation] Proposed staging, offered as proposals only and needing user approval per the project guidelines. Each stage is independently shippable and testable.
  1. **Stage 0, Scenario runner.** Hand-authored tactical scenarios with a results record, which tests the tactical core. This is CMO's model.
  2. **Stage 1, Persistent patrol.** A single boat with persistent damage, weapons, endurance and a simple crew-department state across a linear chain of authored missions, plus a versioned save. This follows Sea Power's "linear/persistent" step.
  3. **Stage 2, Generated patrols.** A mission-template generator plus a traffic director over a regional route graph, with aging contact reports and an abstract bubble spawn and collapse. This reaches roughly Cold Waters or SH3 level.
  4. **Stage 3, Career wrapper.** Reputation, rank, qualifications gating boat classes and mission types, port refit time, and officer promotion and loss.
  5. **Stage 4, Theatre tension.** A global tension variable driving ROE, mission mix and scripted escalation events, with outcomes feeding back. This is the Rule the Waves and Cold Waters level.
  6. **Stage 5, optional and much later.** A semi-autonomous theatre AI that plans opposing force movements, Falcon-style. Treat it as a separate, explicitly approved project.
- [Recommendation] Make campaign-layer code engine-agnostic and data-driven (spawn tables, mission templates and event timelines as Resources or data files). It can then be unit-tested without scenes, and modders can extend it, as happened with SH3 and Cold Waters.
- [Inference] The biggest hidden cost is likely tuning, not code. Falcon's hardest problem was testing whether unscripted AI produced realistic scenarios, and Cold Waters patches fixed "missions with bad parameters." Build tools to fast-forward campaigns headlessly and log outcomes early.

- I found no budget figures for any submarine sim's campaign, and no small-team postmortem specifically about a naval dynamic campaign.
- I could not determine whether Sea Power or Eagle Dynamics have shipped their dynamic campaigns by November 2026.
