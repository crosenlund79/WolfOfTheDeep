# Modern-era naval/submarine simulators as reference games (Dangerous Waters, Cold Waters, Sea Power, Modern Naval Warfare, plus orientation titles)

Research date: 22 November 2026. Research only: this file holds no implementation, assets, code or interface copies. Everything under "Recommendation" is a proposal for the user to judge and does not authorize any work.

**Labels used:** **[Fact]** comes from a page I fetched and read directly. **[Fact-snippet]** comes only from a search-engine summary because the page itself was not fetched or was blocked (HTTP 403). Treat these as lower confidence. **[Estimate]** is a number derived from partial evidence. **[Inference]** is my reasoning. **[Recommendation]** is a proposal only. Store figures (prices, review counts) are snapshots taken on 22 Nov 2026 unless another date is given.

## 1. Dangerous Waters (Sonalysts, 2005) and its lineage (688(I) Hunter/Killer, Sub Command)

### Takeaway
Dangerous Waters (DW) is the reference for station-level fidelity across several platforms. The player can command an SSN, an SSK, a frigate, an MH-60 or a P-3 and work each crew station by hand, including a manual paper-plot style of target motion analysis (TMA), or hand any station to an "autocrew". Hardcore players praise this depth. Accessibility is limited by a manual of roughly 560–570 pages, a missing hands-on tutorial, display clutter and an authored, branching mission campaign with no dynamic career. Its most transferable pattern is the per-station automation toggle.

### Cited Findings
**Production facts**
- [Fact] Developed by Sonalysts Combat Simulations. Published by Strategy First in North America and BlackBean Games in Europe. Sold online through Battlefront.com from 22 Feb 2005, at retail from Dec 2005, on Steam from 7 Feb 2006 and in the EU from 26 May 2006. The full 570-page manual shipped as a PDF. Windows only. — [Wikipedia: Dangerous Waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dangerous_Waters_(video_game))
- [Fact] Metacritic 82/100 ("favorable"). GameSpot 8.3, IGN 8.4, GameZone 8.8, CGW 4/5, GameSpy 4/5, X-Play 3/5, PC Gamer US 85%, PC Gamer UK 82%, PC Zone 73%. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dangerous_Waters_(video_game))
- [Fact] Steam today: $14.99, "Mostly Positive", 79% of 353 user reviews. The store blurb promises to "Take direct control of individual crew stations" and "total control over multiple air, surface, and submarine platforms in a modern-day naval environment." — [Steam: Dangerous Waters](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1600/Dangerous_Waters/)
- [Fact-snippet] Lineage: *Jane's 688(I) Hunter/Killer* was made by Sonalysts and published by EA under the Jane's Combat Simulations label. Its North American release was 27 June 1997; one ComBATsim article says 1998, which conflicts. — [Wikipedia: 688(I) Hunter/Killer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/688(I)_Hunter/Killer). *Sub Command* (Akula/Seawolf/688(I)) was published by EA on 13 Sep 2001, after the Jane's branding was dropped. — [Wikipedia: Sub Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub_Command)
- [Fact] DW's built-in mission editor has a scripting language. It can import scenario scripts from *688(I) Hunter/Killer*, *Sub Command* and *Fleet Command*, so the lineage is technically continuous. A 2009 community mod, *Reinforce Alert*, added new units. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dangerous_Waters_(video_game))
- [Fact] SUBSIM presents DW as the next step after Sub Command, which it calls "an entire leap in advancement" over Jane's 688(I). — [SUBSIM review p1 (5 Mar 2005)](https://www.subsim.com/ssr/dangerous_waters/dangwater_review_page1.htm)

**Multi-platform and station-based play**
- [Fact] Playable units include the Los Angeles (688i) and Seawolf classes, the Akula and Kilo classes, the MH-60, the P-3 Orion and the Oliver Hazard Perry frigate, drawn from the forces of 18 countries. Campaigns can be played as the USN, the Russian Navy or the PLAN. Radar and sonar can be run by hand or handed to AI crew stations. In multiplayer, several players can share one vessel, each manning a crew station. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dangerous_Waters_(video_game))
- [Fact] Each unit has several tactical stations, and "a competent auto-crew can be assigned to each station." — [SUBSIM review p1](https://www.subsim.com/ssr/dangerous_waters/dangwater_review_page1.htm)
- [Fact] Frigate stations: towed array sonar, hull sonar, sonobuoy acoustics, EW, bridge, weapons control and outdoor lookouts. Its two MH-60s are run from the ASTAC station. P-3 stations: EW/MAD, radar, camera turret and sonobuoy acoustics. The P-3 is flown mainly through the task bar ("most players will probably find themselves ordering speed and course via the task bar"), so air units are command-oriented rather than true flight sims. — [SUBSIM review p2](https://www.subsim.com/ssr/dangerous_waters/dangwater_review_page2.htm)
- [Fact] TMA and plotting:
  - Frigate TMA uses a virtual paper plot with rulers, and "frigate TMA can become a nightmare."
  - Contacts can be dropped on the Nav Map without visiting the TMA station, which the reviewer calls an improvement over Sub Command.
  - A "Hide Truth" model means displayed contacts reflect only what sensors and best guesses have established.
  - Bearing lines pile up until "the NavMap looks like a game of Chinese Pick-up Sticks."
  - Auto-TMA can fail to merge one contact's solutions from several sensors, leaving near-duplicates.
  — [SUBSIM review p2](https://www.subsim.com/ssr/dangerous_waters/dangwater_review_page2.htm)
- [Fact-snippet] Autocrew can read sonar displays and plot firing solutions, and players can switch it off for full manual control. — [Worthplaying review (Jun 2005)](https://worthplaying.com/article/2005/6/15/reviews/25244-pc-review-dangerous-waters/)

**Learning curve and accessibility**
- [Fact-snippet] GameSpot (8.3, May 2005): each platform "takes a large time investment to learn," and the game is learnable "if you're willing to read." It calls the 570-page manual excellent. The page fetch was blocked (403). — [GameSpot review](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/dangerous-waters-review/1900-6124232/)
- [Fact] The 562-page manual and about 40 minutes of video tutorials answered Sub Command's "intense learning curve" and "ambiguous manual." — [SUBSIM review p1](https://www.subsim.com/ssr/dangerous_waters/dangwater_review_page1.htm)
- [Fact-snippet] GameChronicles (Oct 2005) calls the learning curve "as steep as a sub's emergency dive" and says the game badly needs a hands-on tutorial. — [GameChronicles](https://www.gamechronicles.com/reviews/pc/dangerouswaters/body.htm). Later Steam users call the curve very steep and say the earlier 688(I) tutorial was dropped. — [Steam user reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1600/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated)

**Scenario and campaign structure**
- [Fact] The campaign has eleven listed missions tied together by a storyline, and earlier outcomes affect later missions. Choosing different platforms expands this to 35 unique missions. "Shifting alliances" mean factions can change intent based on what the player does. — [SUBSIM review p1](https://www.subsim.com/ssr/dangerous_waters/dangwater_review_page1.htm)
- [Fact] SUBSIM also praised the multi-platform teamwork (air units share contacts that trigger ship strikes), Quick Missions and the expanded mission editor. — [SUBSIM review p2](https://www.subsim.com/ssr/dangerous_waters/dangwater_review_page2.htm)

### Inferences
- [Inference] DW's core promise is "be every operator on every platform." This delivers immersion through procedure and tension through ambiguity (the Hide Truth model, manual TMA). It does not offer Silent Hunter-style autonomy (a free patrol in a living war) or a career (persistent boat, crew, rank or renown). No source describes persistent crew or career progression; the campaign is authored and branching.
- [Inference] Per-station autocrew is how DW lets one game serve both novices and purists. The same lever appears in Cold Waters (all stations automated), in Modern Naval Warfare's 2026 accessibility retrofit (Section 4), and in Sea Power's command-level abstraction (Section 3).
- [Inference] Seven platforms, each with several stations, is a scope that only a dedicated studio with a long-running engine lineage could support (688(I) 1997, Sub Command 2001, DW 2005). It is not a precedent a small team should copy.
- [Recommendation] Take from DW the station-plus-autocrew pattern and the "hide truth" philosophy. Do not take multi-platform scope. Treat display clutter (bearing-line pile-up, duplicate solutions) as a known UX hazard to design against.

### Gaps
- Sonalysts' team size and DW's development timeline: no source found.
- The fetched reviews do not document submarine-specific TMA tools (e.g., dot-stack or solution-ranging station details). The SUBSIM review covers frigate and air TMA more than submarine TMA. The manual itself was not consulted.
- LWAMI, a long-running community sensor-model mod for DW, surfaced only in search summaries of SUBSIM forum threads, which describe broadband sensitivity, bearing-error and ESM changes. I could not tie these claims to a specific URL, so they are left out.
- DW patch 1.04 contents: not verified.

## 2. Cold Waters (Killerfish Games, 2017)

### Takeaway
Cold Waters is the clearest small-team commercial precedent. Two full-time developers (a programmer and a lead artist) plus a consulting artist, using Unity 5, shipped a $39.99 game that now holds 87% positive across 3,523 Steam reviews. It deliberately drops station simulation: there are no stations, no waterfall and the AI crew does the TMA. In their place it offers a 3D external view, a tactical map and a strategic-map campaign with randomised missions, while keeping a sonar model whose tactics (layers, convergence zones, bottom bounce) are valid. Crew are not modelled as people. A large community modding scene, including the Epic Mod, extended the game well after official support ended.

### Cited Findings
**Production facts**
- [Fact] Developed and published by Killerfish Games. Steam lists the release as 5 Jun 2017. $39.99. "Very Positive", 87% of 3,523 English reviews. "Includes level editor". Billed as "Spiritual Successor to the Microprose Classic 'Red Storm Rising'". — [Steam: Cold Waters](https://store.steampowered.com/app/541210/Cold_Waters/)
- [Fact] Wikipedia gives 5 July 2017, which conflicts with Steam. SUBSIM's review is dated 10 June 2017, and its 14 June update covers patch v1.02, which supports June. Wikipedia lists Windows and macOS (a PS4 category tag also appears but was not verified). — [Wikipedia: Cold Waters (video game)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_Waters_(video_game)); [SUBSIM review](https://www.subsim.com/ssr/cold_waters/review_cold_waters.php)
- [Fact] Team, from a Rock Paper Shotgun interview with co-founder Paul Sincock (reposted April 2017): "There's myself (programmer) and Nils Dücker (lead artist) full time." Przemek Starkiewicz is a consulting artist working mostly on 2D art and effects. Music and other tasks the core group lacked time or expertise for were outsourced. — [RPS interview via CombatACE](https://combatace.com/forums/topic/90480-rock-paper-shotgun-interview-with-cold-waters/)
- [Fact] The studio's previous game, *Atlantic Fleet* (60+ ship classes), took about six months of extra work to port to PC. It was underpriced on PC, which hurt funding for its expansion, so Cold Waters was priced as a full PC game "with a dynamic campaign and mod support." — [RPS interview via CombatACE](https://combatace.com/forums/topic/90480-rock-paper-shotgun-interview-with-cold-waters/)
- [Fact-snippet] Engine: Unity 5 (PCGamingWiki; the fetch was blocked with 403) — [PCGamingWiki](https://www.pcgamingwiki.com/wiki/Cold_Waters); also stated in a user review — [Medium](https://medium.com/@BasaltOnFire/how-great-is-cold-waters-5e8062f1367c)
- [Fact] Studio split: Triassic Games' CEO "Nils" says he co-founded Killerfish in 2013–14 with Paul Sincock, who "did not desire continuing on with the team that built Atlantic Fleet and Cold Waters." Nils secured the rights to the Killerfish backlog assets in early 2019, "which is when we seriously started developing Sea Power." (Steam post, 7 Jul 2020.) — [Triassic Games Steam post](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1286220/discussions/0/2565312892653650863)

**Design compromises: the 3D action-sim approach**
- [Fact] Red Storm Rising "centred on your decisions as a commander, rather than the technical operation of various stations," and Killerfish kept that focus. There is no station simulation and no first-person interior. Combat runs through the periscope view, a tactical map, and damage-control and weapon-loadout screens. — [RPS interview via CombatACE](https://combatace.com/forums/topic/90480-rock-paper-shotgun-interview-with-cold-waters/)
- [Fact] The sonar model covers thermal layers, convergence zones, bottom bounce, ambient noise "and more." There is no waterfall display, but "the tactics associated with underwater acoustics and sonar detection are indeed valid." — [RPS interview via CombatACE](https://combatace.com/forums/topic/90480-rock-paper-shotgun-interview-with-cold-waters/)
- [Fact] "You do not have to struggle with the nuts and bolts of TMA and sonar"; "your AI crew will do most of the pencil-whipping for you." Contacts on the 2D tactical map are "only as reliable as the sonar and visual detection can make them." Optional signature-classification tools can speed up a solution, and Elite difficulty turns off auto-classification. — [SUBSIM review](https://www.subsim.com/ssr/cold_waters/review_cold_waters.php)
- [Fact] Crew: "crew abilities, health, and morale" do not affect gameplay. Ships and crews are treated "as individual capable fighting units." — [RPS interview via CombatACE](https://combatace.com/forums/topic/90480-rock-paper-shotgun-interview-with-cold-waters/)
- [Fact] Damage: running at flank speed with the scope up damaged it, it could not be lowered, and "repairs could only be made in port." At launch, course and depth were hotkey-only (WASD) with a helm station still in development, the message bar kept no history, and there were no crew voices. — [SUBSIM review](https://www.subsim.com/ssr/cold_waters/review_cold_waters.php)
- [Fact] Weapons include wire-guided torpedoes and anti-ship and cruise missiles. The torpedoes are era-appropriate: the MK16 straight runner, the slow MK37 that needs point-blank range, and the MK48. There are 40+ researched ship and submarine classes. — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/541210/Cold_Waters/); [SUBSIM review](https://www.subsim.com/ssr/cold_waters/review_cold_waters.php)
- [Fact] Playable boats at release were the Skipjack, Permit, Sturgeon and Los Angeles classes. Sincock argued that four boats give "a much greater variety and depth of experiences" than Atlantic Fleet's 60+ classes. — [RPS interview via CombatACE](https://combatace.com/forums/topic/90480-rock-paper-shotgun-interview-with-cold-waters/)

**Campaign structure: dynamic war progression and mission generation**
- [Fact] The campaign opens on a strategic map of the theatre, where aircraft, satellites, tanks, ships and subs move in accelerated time. News flashes report the US/NATO–Soviet war, and missions are time-sensitive. Single missions contain "a generous dose of randomness to keep them replayable": ocean conditions and the number, type and position of enemies vary between starts. — [SUBSIM review](https://www.subsim.com/ssr/cold_waters/review_cold_waters.php)
- [Fact] The store bills a "Dynamic Campaign where your performance matters," with missions such as "intercepting convoys, amphibious landings, insertion missions." — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/541210/Cold_Waters/)
- [Fact] Unlike the Red Storm Rising novel, which ends in diplomacy, the campaign "can deteriorate into global thermonuclear war." — [RPS interview via CombatACE](https://combatace.com/forums/topic/90480-rock-paper-shotgun-interview-with-cold-waters/)
- [Fact-snippet] On the strategic map the player's submarine has three movement speeds, and enemy task forces appear as icons once detected by reconnaissance or proximity. — [Magic Game World strategic map guide](https://www.magicgameworld.com/cold-waters-strategic-map-guide/)
- [Fact] Era coverage changed after launch. SUBSIM (June 2017) describes 1968 and 1984 campaigns. Wikipedia now lists 1968, 1984 or 2000 against the USSR or the PRC. A community wiki lists a 2000 South China Sea scenario. — [SUBSIM review](https://www.subsim.com/ssr/cold_waters/review_cold_waters.php); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_Waters_(video_game)); [PMC Tactical: Campaign](https://cold-waters.pmctactical.org/campaign.php)
- [Fact] Sincock on dynamic campaigns: they are hard to build well, but can work "as long as it is balanced, immersive and provides compelling choices for the player." Over-ambitious dynamic campaigns are an easy trap. Multiplayer and monetised designs offer higher returns for less risk. — [RPS interview via CombatACE](https://combatace.com/forums/topic/90480-rock-paper-shotgun-interview-with-cold-waters/)

**Reception**
- [Fact] SUBSIM (Neal Stevens, 10 Jun 2017): "Cold Waters is a truly great game." It praised the AI ("very sound and reasonable AI and tactics"), the moddable data files and the time compression. It criticised the over-capable Soviet aircraft ("Tu-142 Bear bombers will get called in on almost every mission"), dead hulls that kept emitting sonar signatures and the absence of neutral shipping. — [SUBSIM review](https://www.subsim.com/ssr/cold_waters/review_cold_waters.php)
- [Fact] PC Gamer (Matt Elliott, 7 Jul 2017): "An excellent stealth game set under the sea." — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/cold-waters-is-a-tense-focused-take-on-submarine-combat). [Fact-snippet] The body text, seen only in a search summary, says the game eases players into complex systems, calls it a streamlined successor to Red Storm Rising and finds the 1968 campaign the tougher of the two.
- [Fact-snippet] Metacritic had too few critic reviews for a score (one review); the user score was 8.2. — [Metacritic](https://legacy.prod.metacritic.com/game/pc/cold-waters)

**Modding (including the Epic Mod)**
- [Fact] "all of the vessels' characteristics are stored in text files." — [SUBSIM review](https://www.subsim.com/ssr/cold_waters/review_cold_waters.php). Sincock says mod support has been central from the start. — [RPS interview via CombatACE](https://combatace.com/forums/topic/90480-rock-paper-shotgun-interview-with-cold-waters/)
- [Fact-snippet] Killerfish's modding guide says the game loads content from an *override* folder first, then *default*, then *internal*. It also documents a 4096×2048 world map and lists mission titles with map coordinates. — [CombatACE: Cold Waters modding guide](https://combatace.com/forums/topic/91455-modding-guide-creating-custom-content-for-cold-waters-by-killerfish-games-cold-waters/)
- [Fact-snippet] The Epic Mod is described as "aimed at expanding and improving the original game for a more interesting and multi-faceted gameplay." Its ModDB page links a readme and install tutorial, but the page itself returned 403. — [ModDB: Cold Waters Epic Mod](https://www.moddb.com/mods/cold-waters-epic-mod)
- [Fact-snippet] Other large community projects include "New Playable Subs & More" — [ModDB](https://www.moddb.com/mods/new-playable-subs-more) — and a Soviet-side campaign mod (work in progress) for North Atlantic 1984 and 1968. Its playable boats are the Victor I/II/III, Alfa, Sierra, Oscar, Charlie I/II, November, Echo II and Foxtrot. — [SUBSIM forum t-232230](https://www.subsim.com/radioroom/archive/index.php/t-232230.html)
- [Fact-snippet] When it announced *War on the Sea*, Killerfish said it would stop updating Cold Waters, Atlantic Fleet and Pacific Fleet. — [Mudspike forum](https://forums.mudspike.com/t/new-game-war-on-the-sea-from-the-cold-waters-team/10113)

### Inferences
- [Inference] Cold Waters shows that players accept fully automated TMA and sonar if the acoustic environment still drives tactical decisions (layer, speed, aspect, convergence zone). Tension comes from stealth and from evading torpedoes in 3D, not from operating instruments.
- [Inference] Compared with Silent Hunter's pillars:
  - **Tension:** strong.
  - **Immersion:** weak. There is no interior, crew are not people, and there were no voices at launch.
  - **Autonomy:** partial. The submarine moves on the strategic map, but play centres on mission "drops."
  - **Dynamic career:** missing. There is no named crew, rank or boat progression, and crew health and morale are explicitly out.
- [Inference] The long-lived mod scene (playable-sub packs, a Soviet campaign, the Epic Mod's unit expansion) points to the base game's scope gaps: few playable boats, a US-only player side and limited unit variety. It also shows how plain-text, data-driven units multiply a small team's content.
- [Recommendation] The *override → default → internal* load order is a direct precedent for this project's planned runtime `custom/` override resolver (guideline 3). It is worth citing in any asset or data-architecture discussion.
- [Recommendation] Sincock's warning about dynamic-campaign scope, together with Sea Power's slippage (Section 3), supports starting with a constrained generator: randomised missions inside a simple persistent war state.

### Gaps
- No Epic Mod changelog, playable-boat list or current version could be verified (ModDB returned 403). A Steam thread titled "Epic Mod 2.55" appeared in search results, but I could not tie the version or date to a verified URL.
- No source explains the internal rules of Cold Waters' war progression, such as how mission results move the front or how missions are weighted. A Steam forum claim that "every mission is from a random mission generator" could not be tied to a specific thread.
- Cold Waters' development start date and unit sales: not found.

## 3. Sea Power: Naval Combat in the Missile Age (Triassic Games / MicroProse)

### Takeaway
As of November 2026 Sea Power is still in Early Access, which began on 12 Nov 2024, and is commercially strong at 91% positive across 4,292 reviews. It is a Cold War (1960s–80s) task-force and unit-command tactical simulator built in Unity by the former Cold Waters team. It models acoustics as a sound-speed-profile and layer-based detection model that the player does not see directly; there is no crew-station or captain-level submarine experience. Its 1.0 release and Dynamic Campaign have slipped repeatedly: from Q2 2025, to mid-2026, to "Phase 1 at end of 2026", and to 2027 per an October 2026 report, while the Steam page still says Q4 2026. This is the strongest available warning about dynamic-campaign scope for a small, experienced team.

### Cited Findings
**Production facts and status**
- [Fact] Developed by Triassic Games and published by MicroProse Software. Early Access began 12 Nov 2024. $49.99 (on sale at $39.99 until 25 Nov 2026). "Very Positive", 91% of 4,292 English reviews. Recent reviews (30 days): "Overwhelmingly Positive", 96% of 313. "No price increase is planned at this time." — [Steam: Sea Power](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1286220/Sea_Power__Naval_Combat_in_the_Missile_Age/)
- [Fact] Engine: Unity, per the Wikipedia infobox. Platform: Windows. Work began in 2018. "Feature creep and an overly-optimistic plan" led to an Early Access release instead of a full release. It is the studio's debut title and was overseen by Cold Waters' lead designer. MicroProse announced it would publish in 2020. — [Wikipedia: Sea Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Power:_Naval_Combat_in_the_Missile_Age)
- [Fact] There is a minor conflict on the start date: the CEO says serious development began in early 2019, after he secured the Killerfish backlog assets. — [Triassic Steam post (7 Jul 2020)](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1286220/discussions/0/2565312892653650863)
- [Fact] Reception: it was among Steam's best-selling games in November 2024, and 90% of Steam reviewers were positive in May 2026. The UK Defence Journal praised its immersive small details. CIMSEC called it a "useful wargaming tool." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Power:_Naval_Combat_in_the_Missile_Age)
- [Fact] Version history: v0.7.0 (Feb 2026) added a new campaign and improved graphics. v0.8.0 (Jul 2026) added the *Pacific Strike '85* campaign and Task Force Mode. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Power:_Naval_Combat_in_the_Missile_Age)
- [Fact] The Royal Navy and Argentina factions, with South Atlantic 1982 campaigns, were scheduled for 11 Nov 2026. — [Simulation Daily (20 Oct 2026)](https://simulationdaily.com/news/sea-power-royal-navy-argentina/)

**Dynamic campaign and 1.0 slippage (dated sources, in order)**
- [Fact] Nov 2025 roadmap: Dynamic Campaign in Q2 2026, improvements and full release in Q3 2026. — [Simulation Daily (15 Nov 2025)](https://simulationdaily.com/news/sea-power-early-access-roadmap/)
- [Fact] The June 2026 developer report has several relevant statements:
  - "Dynamic Campaign Phase 1 is now planned for the end of 2026." It depends on AI behaviour, force persistence, logistics and map support.
  - AI work "has had to take a back seat at times while the map reprojection project moves forward." The reprojection moves the strategic map to a 3D globe.
  - "Royal Navy release is moving to September 2026."
  - Performance and stability work (save/load, long sessions) "continued throughout 2026."
  — [MicroProse: Sea Power Development Report, June 2026](https://www.microprose.com/news/sea-power/sea-power-development-report-june-2026)
- [Fact] Oct 2026: the game "was expected in Q3 2026, but it was then delayed to 2027 alongside the much-awaited fully dynamic campaign mode." — [Simulation Daily (20 Oct 2026)](https://simulationdaily.com/news/sea-power-royal-navy-argentina/). **This conflicts with** the Steam page, which still says "We are currently targeting a 1.0 release in Q4 2026" and describes a theatre-scale Dynamic Campaign "beta… mid 2026," which is evidently stale text. — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1286220/Sea_Power__Naval_Combat_in_the_Missile_Age/)

**How submarines, sensors, acoustics and weapons are modelled**
- [Fact] Setting and scope: 1960s–1980s, mainly NATO versus the Warsaw Pact. 200+ units, including never-built designs. Players can command fleets or directly control units ("selecting targets or activating sensors"). Combat can span large regions and several in-game days with time compression. Sensors are central to "overcoming fog of war" without hitting neutrals or exposing your own units. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Power:_Naval_Combat_in_the_Missile_Age)
- [Fact] "Advanced sensor modeling for radar, sonar, electronic countermeasures, and visual identification"; 150+ weapon systems; "Advanced flight and ship physics modeling." — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1286220/Sea_Power__Naval_Combat_in_the_Missile_Age/)
- [Fact] Developer dev diary on torpedoes (29 Jan 2022):
  - "Noise produced or reflected off of a target is dependent on the aspect and distance to the target."
  - "Transmission then depends on the local speed sound profile (SSP), alongside layer and surface duct effects."
  - "each torpedo has its own detection threshold."
  - Torpedoes use passive or active seekers with proportional-navigation guidance.
  - Noisemakers and self-propelled decoys create false targets, and each torpedo has a tech-level-based chance to reject them.
  - Wake homing was "to come soon." Wire guidance and player torpedo control are not described.
  — [Triassic Dev Diary #6](https://www.triassicgames.com/2022/01/29/dev-diary-6-torpedoes-and-counter-measures/)
- [Fact] A community sonar guide, explicitly written before the "0.8.0 sonar rework" and so dated:
  - Hull sonar cannot see below the thermal layer; "we must use a VDS or towed array."
  - A contact in the shadow zone receives "a massive -1000db penalty."
  - "In Sea Power, those distances are 30nm and 60nm" for convergence zones in deep water.
  - Bottom bounce in shallow water is fixed at about 15 nm.
  - Speed and cavitation raise noise.
  - A contact appears once the computed value exceeds about 5 dB.
  - The player cannot see the detection equation: "we cant see the exact equation the game is using."
  - The guide does not cover TMA or firing solutions.
  — [Steam guide: Comprehensive Guide to Sonar in Sea Power (outdated as of 0.8.0)](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3654466303)
- [Fact] The June 2026 report confirms UK submarine content (Oberon, Porpoise, Churchill, Swiftsure, Trafalgar, Valiant) and shows dipping-sonar ASW. Its images include HMS Tireless "firing Tigerfish." — [MicroProse June 2026 report](https://www.microprose.com/news/sea-power/sea-power-development-report-june-2026)

**Campaigns, editor and modding**
- [Fact] The current campaigns are linear, with branching options and story events. ## 6. Cross-title synthesis: strengths, compromises and gaps against Silent Hunter's pillars, and production precedents for a small team

### Takeaway
The four titles sit on a fidelity-versus-accessibility spectrum: Cold Waters (abstracted, 3D), Sea Power (unit and task-force command), DW (multi-platform stations with autocrew) and MNW (single-boat, full-fidelity stations). **None delivers Silent Hunter's combination of a free-roaming patrol, a dynamic war and a persistent career with a crew you care about.** The best-received (Cold Waters, Sea Power) are the ones that abstracted station work and shipped a complete, fair-fighting core loop. The worst-received (MNW) maximised instrument fidelity and launched without an opponent.

### Inferences
**Strengths, compromises and gaps against Silent Hunter's tension, immersion, autonomy and dynamic career.** All entries in this subsection are [Inference] drawn from the cited findings above.

- **Dangerous Waters**
  - *Strengths:* procedural immersion; true manual TMA; hidden truth; multi-platform ASW interplay; per-station autocrew; multi-crew multiplayer.
  - *Compromises:* a manual of about 570 pages; no hands-on tutorial; Nav Map clutter; air units only command-level.
  - *Gaps vs SH:* the campaign is authored and branching, with no free patrol, no persistent boat, crew or career, and no walkable interior. Tension is high, but it comes from cognitive load rather than narrative stakes.
- **Cold Waters**
  - *Strengths:* readable 3D tension (torpedo evasion, layers, convergence zones); a quick-to-learn loop; a strategic-map campaign with randomised missions and an escalating war; plain-text moddability; tiny-team economics.
  - *Compromises:* no stations or waterfall; AI does the TMA; crew are not people; repairs only in port; hotkey helm at launch.
  - *Gaps vs SH:* weak immersion (no interior, no named crew) and no career progression. Autonomy is limited to strategic-map moves between mission drops. The US-only side and few playable boats were filled in by mods.
- **Sea Power**
  - *Strengths:* a broad, realistic sensor and weapon ecosystem driven by an SSP and layer model; task-force scale; a strong editor and Workshop; high review scores.
  - *Compromises:* the submarine is a unit, not a captain's world; the detection maths is hidden from players; AI and campaign depth lag.
  - *Gaps vs SH:* no crew or interior, no single-boat patrol fantasy and no career. The dynamic campaign is still unshipped two years into EA.
- **Modern Naval Warfare**
  - *Strengths:* the deepest modern SSN instrument set (BB, NB, DEMON, LOFAR, TMA rulers, flooding permeability); a single-boat focus; a planned co-op crew; a CIC interior.
  - *Compromises:* hardcore-first onboarding (a "Dark Souls" reputation); launched without enemy combat AI; heavy hardware requirements; $60 EA price.
  - *Gaps vs SH:* the campaign is 18 authored missions with no dynamic war or career. Tension is undermined without an opponent that fights back. Accessibility is being retrofitted.
- **Command: Modern Operations**
  - *Strengths:* data breadth and operational context.
  - *Gaps vs SH:* no captain-level immersion at all.

**Synthesised lessons for a small Godot team**
- [Recommendation] Scope to **one modern SSN class** and a **layered fidelity model**. The default is a Cold Waters-style tactical map and 3D view. Optional stations (sonar waterfall, TMA plot) sit on top, each with a per-station autocrew toggle (DW). Assistance or "show truth" options should exist from day one, not be retrofitted as MNW had to.
- [Recommendation] Make **opponent AI and a fair-fighting core loop** the first acceptance gate, ahead of instrument depth (lesson from MNW).
- [Recommendation] Treat the **dynamic campaign and career as staged scope**. Start with a persistent war state plus randomised mission generation (Cold Waters), then add persistence and logistics later. Plan explicitly against Sea Power-style slippage and Sincock's warning about over-ambitious dynamic campaigns.
- [Recommendation] Use **data-driven, text-based unit and sensor definitions** with an override-folder load order (the Cold Waters precedent). This aligns with this project's own `custom/` override requirement and its modding potential.
- [Recommendation] The career and crew pillar is the clearest **market gap** among modern-era titles. Crew-as-people mechanics are currently proven only in WWII or party titles (Uboat, S.O.N.A.R.) and are planned in Nuclear Sub.
- [Inference] Pricing precedents for this niche run from $39.99 (Cold Waters) to $49.99 (Sea Power) to $59.99 (MNW). The $59.99 price drew value complaints for an incomplete EA build.
- [Inference] Timeline precedents: even experienced or well-staffed teams took about 5–6+ years to reach EA (Sea Power, MNW). Cold Waters, a narrow-scope game from a team that had already shipped Atlantic Fleet on an existing toolset, is the only fast small-team precedent found, although its exact development span was not found.

### Gaps
- No source gives Cold Waters' development duration, so the "fast small team" precedent is qualitative.
- Sales figures (units or revenue) are not published for any of the four main titles. Review counts are the only proxy found.
- No direct developer statements were found comparing any of these titles with Silent Hunter. The comparisons against SH's pillars above are my analysis.
